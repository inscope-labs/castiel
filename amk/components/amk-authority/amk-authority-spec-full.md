## Core Intent

*Informative overview. The normative sections below remain authoritative wherever a requirement is already defined.*

- **Make claims verifiable, not unlimited.** The Authority issues or validates attestations (issuer, subject, claim/role, scope, validity, epoch, key binding, proof of possession) whose meaning is strictly bounded. A valid signature never implies truth or trust beyond that defined meaning.
- **Attest only; never decide or act.** Attestation, authentication, authorization, and enforcement stay logically distinct, even when co-located. The Authority does not grant permission, execute tasks, run the Host AI, route sessions, or bypass the controller, which alone applies policy and enforces.
- **No self-minting or elevation.** The Host AI, console, tasks, packages, adapters, and external inputs may carry or request attestations but cannot mint, alter, or escalate them. Authority material never overrides kernel invariants, controller policy, or higher governance.
- **Fail closed, with explicit validity.** Unverifiable, ambiguous, stale, expired, revoked, malformed, or scope-mismatched attestations are never accepted as current. Outcomes when revocation status is unknown are set by policy (deny, suspend, restrict), never by a permissive fallback, and revocation never rewrites audit history.
- **Minimal, scoped identity.** Disclose only what verification needs. Keep session, entity, agent, and gateway identities distinct. There is no separate user identity: the console, as an entity, is the sole representative of the operator. Keep secrets and personal data out of logs.
- **Platform-independent and conformance-tested.** This contract is independent of any Host AI, OS, platform or language. Implementations declare algorithms, trust anchors, and failure behavior; optional features cannot weaken mandatory checks; conformance tests cover valid, malformed, expired, revoked, mismatched, and untrusted attestations.

**Essence:** the Authority proves who or what is participating, within bounded and verifiable limits, and leaves every decision about what they may do to the controller.

## Terms used in this document

Definitions in this section are authoritative for this specification.

- **Attestation Authority (AA):** the sole issuer of role attestations. This component.
- **Role attestation:** a signed statement binding a session public key to a role for a session handle and epoch.
- **Session public key:** the public key of a key pair generated by a session participant (entity, console, or controller).
- **Attestation client:** any AMK core entity or session participant that requests, presents, or verifies an attestation.
- **Session handle:** the unique identifier the controller assigns to a session.
- **Epoch:** a monotonic counter within a session, incremented on re-key or role change. Each epoch has a start and an end, defined by the controller and supplied to the Authority in the session record.
- **Grace window (G):** the period after epoch end during which an attestation remains valid.
- **Revocation list (RL):** a signed, timestamped list of revoked attestations.
- **`RL_INTERVAL`:** the revocation list publication interval (`DV-ATA-06`).
- **`R_MAX`:** the maximum age of a cached revocation list before it is considered stale (`DV-ATA-02`).
- **Session record:** the controller-supplied record of a session's handle, epoch, epoch start and end, and declared roles (`ATA-02:02`).
- **Root key:** the pinned key that signs only the Authority's metadata document (`ATA-01:07`).
- **Signing key:** a key listed in the signed metadata, used to sign attestations and revocation lists; identified by `kid`.
- **Metadata document:** the signed document published at `/.well-known/amk-authority` (`ATA-01:07`).
- **Presentation proof:** the presenter's signature, over a verifier-supplied nonce, that it holds the session private key (`ATA-03:04`).
- **Profile:** one of Basic, Standard, or High, defining the security requirements for the Authority's operation.
- **Proof-of-possession (PoP):** a demonstration that the requester holds the private key corresponding to the session public key.

## ATA-1: Role

- The Attestation Authority is the sole issuer of **role attestations** for the AMK ecosystem.
- A role attestation cryptographically binds:
  - a session public key,
  - to a role (an arbitrary string declared in the session's Init Envelope),
  - for a specific session handle and epoch,
  - within a validity window (`valid_from` / `valid_to`).
- The Authority never issues long-term identity certificates to the AMK core entities or other session participants. Long-term identity, if supported, stays inside the Authority and is never revealed to the attestation clients.
- The Authority does not know or care how the attestation clients are implemented. It treats all clients as black boxes.

## ATA-2: Platform

- The Authority is language- and platform-agnostic. Its interface is envelope- and structured-message-oriented.
- It has zero UI/UX; all interaction is programmatic.
- The Authority MAY run as a standalone service, a library, or an embedded component, provided it meets the profile requirements of `ATA-06`.

## ATA-3: Trust

- Trusted for the statements it signs.
- Untrusted for enforcement: the controller enforces. The Authority's attestations are inputs to enforcement, not enforcement itself.
- Clients verify attestations against signing keys in the Authority's signed metadata, which they verify against a pinned root key.
- The Authority and its clients do not trust each other blindly: clients verify signatures; the Authority verifies proof-of-possession before issuance.

## ATA-4: Self-Containment

- The Authority only defines:
  - the attestation format (`ATA-01`),
  - issuance and verification rules (`ATA-02`, `ATA-03`),
  - revocation semantics (`ATA-04`),
  - identity binding rules when applicable (`ATA-05`),
  - profile requirements (`ATA-06`),
  - and the cryptographic obligations on its own signing keys.
- It does not define or constrain the behavior of AMK components beyond the attestation interface.

## ATA-5: Invariants

- INV-ATA-01: The Authority never issues a role attestation without proof-of-possession of the session private key.
- INV-ATA-02: The Authority never issues an attestation for a role outside the session's declared role list, which it obtains only from the controller's session record. No session record, no attestation.
- INV-ATA-03: The Authority never reveals long-term identity to any session participant.
- INV-ATA-04: A failed verification is an opaque denial; clients do not distinguish failure causes to unauthenticated parties.
- INV-ATA-05: Revocation status is checked on every verification; unavailable or stale revocation data is never treated as "not revoked".
- INV-ATA-06: The Authority never issues an attestation with `valid_from` before the start of the epoch or `valid_to` beyond the end of the epoch plus the grace window `G`.
- INV-ATA-07: Only attestations signed by a key that the signed metadata lists as `active` or `retired` are accepted.

It never issues long-term identity certificates to the AMK Components / Entities. Three operational profiles define its security requirements.

### ATA-00:01 Role

- The Attestation Authority is the sole issuer of role attestations.
- A role attestation binds a session public key to a role for a specific session handle and epoch, within a validity window.
- The Authority never issues long-term identity certificates to the AMK core entities. Identity binding, if supported, stays internal.

### ATA-00:02 Trust Boundary

- Trusted for the statements it signs.
- Untrusted for enforcement: the controller enforces.
- Clients verify attestations against signing keys published in the Authority's signed metadata, which they verify against a pinned root key (`ATA-01:07`).
- The Authority verifies proof-of-possession before issuance.

### ATA-00:03 Self-Containment

- The Authority only defines the attestation format, issuance and verification rules, revocation semantics, and its own key obligations.
- It does not know or care how clients are implemented.

### ATA-00:04 Invariants

- INV-ATA-01: No attestation without proof-of-possession of the session private key.
- INV-ATA-02: No attestation for a role outside the session's declared role list, which the Authority obtains only from the controller's session record (`ATA-02:02`). No session record, no attestation.
- INV-ATA-03: Long-term identity is never revealed to session participants.
- INV-ATA-04: Failed verification is opaque denial; clients do not distinguish failure causes to unauthenticated parties.
- INV-ATA-05: Revocation status is checked on every verification. Unavailable or stale revocation data is never treated as "not revoked".
- INV-ATA-06: `valid_from` is never before the start of the epoch, and `valid_to` never exceeds the end of the epoch plus grace window `G`.
- INV-ATA-07: Only attestations signed by a key that the signed metadata lists as `active` or `retired` are accepted.

### ATA-00:05 Relationship to Other Components

- amk-controller: consumes attestations; verifies them under ATA-03 before relying on them and decides their effect under its own policy. It also supplies the session record (`ATA-02:02`).
- amk-console: no direct interaction.
- Entities: request attestations for assumed roles.

### ATA-00:06 Summary

- The Authority is a self-contained, language-agnostic issuer of role attestations. It is trusted only for what it signs. Enforcement belongs elsewhere.

## 01-attestation-format.md

**File:** `01-attestation-format.md`
**Scope:** Attestation structure, serialization, signing keys, metadata, validity rules.
**Reference prefix:** `ATA-01`

---

### ATA-01:01 Structure

- A role attestation is a JSON object (RFC 8259, UTF-8). It MUST contain the following fields:
  - `issuer`: the Authority's identifier (an HTTPS URL).
  - `kid`: the identifier of the signing key (`ATA-01:07`).
  - `subject_public_key`: the session public key.
  - `role`: the role string.
  - `session_handle`: the session handle.
  - `epoch`: the epoch number (non-negative integer).
  - `valid_from`: the start of the validity window.
  - `valid_to`: the end of the validity window.
  - `nonce`: a unique nonce for this attestation. It is also the attestation's identifier in revocation lists (`ATA-04`).
  - `signature`: the Authority's signature over the canonical serialization.
- Binary values in every Authority structure are base64url without padding (RFC 4648, section 5). Timestamps are RFC 3339 in UTC with a `Z` suffix.
- No other fields are permitted unless a profile defines them. Verifiers MUST reject attestations that contain undefined fields.

### ATA-01:02 Canonical Serialization

- The signature MUST be computed over the JSON Canonicalization Scheme (JCS, RFC 8785) serialization of the attestation object without the `signature` field.
- All numeric fields are integers.

### ATA-01:03 Validity Window

- `valid_from` MUST be less than or equal to `valid_to`.
- `valid_from` MUST NOT precede the start of the epoch, and `valid_to` MUST NOT exceed the end of the epoch plus the grace window `G` (`DV-ATA-01`). [INV-ATA-06]
- The window length MUST NOT exceed the maximum validity window (`DV-ATA-03`).
- Epoch start and end MUST come from the controller's session record (`ATA-02:02`), never from the requester.
- The Authority MUST refuse to issue an attestation with an invalid window.

### ATA-01:04 Nonce

- The `nonce` field MUST be unique per attestation.
- The nonce MUST be generated by the Authority using a cryptographically secure random source.
- The nonce length MUST be at least 128 bits.

### ATA-01:05 Signature

- The mandatory-to-implement signature algorithm is Ed25519 (RFC 8032).
- Another algorithm MAY be used only if the signed metadata lists it for the signing key and the profile permits it.
- The algorithm is taken from the metadata entry for the attestation's `kid`, never from the attestation itself.
- Clients MUST reject attestations whose key has an unknown or unsupported algorithm.

### ATA-01:06 Example

- Reserved. Non-normative test vectors will be published with the reference implementation. This ID is kept so references remain valid.

### ATA-01:07 Signing Keys and Metadata

- The Authority MUST publish a signed metadata document at `/.well-known/amk-authority` on its issuer origin (RFC 8615), over HTTPS, as `application/json`.
- The document MUST contain: `issuer`, `metadata_version` (integer that never decreases), `published_at`, `profile` (`basic`, `standard` or `high`), `root_kid`, `signing_keys`, `revocation_list_url`, `signature`.
- It MUST also contain `revocation_status_url` under High, MAY contain it under Standard, and MUST contain `client_authentication` and `evidence` where `ATA-02:05` and `ATA-06:04` require them.
- Each entry of `signing_keys` MUST contain `kid`, `alg`, `public_key`, `not_before`, `not_after` and `status`. `status` is `active`, `retired` or `revoked`.
- `kid` is the base64url SHA-256 JWK Thumbprint (RFC 7638, OKP key members as in RFC 8037) of the public key.
- The Authority MUST have one root key. The root key signs metadata documents only (JCS, `ATA-01:02`) and never attestations or revocation lists.
- Clients pin the root public key (and `issuer`) out of band at deployment. A client MAY pin more than one root key during a root rotation. Root rotation is out-of-band re-pinning.
- The Authority signs attestations and revocation lists only with a key whose `status` is `active`. A `retired` key signs nothing new; attestations it signed stay valid until their own `valid_to`. A `revoked` key's signatures MUST be rejected.
- Rotation: publish the new key as `active`, then mark the old key `retired` at the switch. The old key MAY be removed once every attestation it signed has expired. A compromised key is marked `revoked`.
- Clients MUST verify the metadata signature with a pinned root key, MUST reject a `metadata_version` lower than the last one they saw, and MUST reject an attestation whose `kid` is absent from the metadata or whose key status is `revoked`.
- Each `kid` in `signing_keys` MUST be unique, and MUST equal the thumbprint of its `public_key`. Clients MUST reject a metadata document that lists a `kid` more than once or whose `kid` does not match its key, and MUST NOT guess between ambiguous keys.
- Clients MUST refresh metadata when their cached copy is older than `DV-ATA-08`, and MUST reject attestations when that refresh fails and the cached copy is still older than `DV-ATA-08`.
- Servers SHOULD set `Cache-Control: max-age` no larger than `DV-ATA-08` (RFC 9111).

### ATA-01:08 Summary

- An attestation is a JCS-canonical JSON object signed with Ed25519 by a key listed in signed metadata. Its window lies inside the controller-defined epoch plus the grace window. Keys, profile, and endpoints are published in signed metadata verified against a pinned root key.

## 02-issuance.md

**File:** `02-issuance.md`
**Scope:** Issuance protocol, proof-of-possession, binding rules.
**Reference prefix:** `ATA-02`

---

### ATA-02:01 Issuance Request

- A client requests an attestation by sending an issuance request (JSON, UTF-8) over TLS 1.3 (RFC 8446).
- The request MUST contain:
  - `session_public_key`: the public key to bind.
  - `role`: the requested role.
  - `session_handle`: the session handle.
  - `epoch`: the epoch number.
  - `proof_of_possession`: a PoP as defined in `ATA-02:03`.
  - `request_nonce`: a unique nonce for the request (at least 128 bits).
  - `request_timestamp`: the request time.
- Session keys are Ed25519 (RFC 8032) unless a profile defines another algorithm.
- The request carries no role list and no epoch boundaries. Both come from the session record (`ATA-02:02`).

### ATA-02:02 Request Validation

- The Authority MUST validate:
  - the request format,
  - that the request nonce is fresh (within `DV-ATA-04`) and has not been seen in that window; the Authority MUST remember request nonces for the window,
  - that the request timestamp is within the freshness window plus clock skew (`DV-ATA-07`),
  - that a session record exists for the `session_handle` and `epoch`, and the epoch has not ended beyond `G`.
- The session record is a JSON object with `session_handle`, `epoch`, `epoch_start`, `epoch_end` and `declared_roles`. `declared_roles` is an array of unique, non-empty UTF-8 strings of at most 256 bytes each.
- The Authority MUST accept session records only from the pinned controller identity over an authenticated channel, and MUST NOT take a role list or epoch boundaries from a requester.
- A session record is immutable per (`session_handle`, `epoch`). A different record for the same pair MUST be rejected.
- The Authority MUST rate-limit issuance requests per source.
- On any failure, the Authority MUST reject the request with an opaque error.

### ATA-02:03 Proof-of-Possession

- The requester MUST prove possession of the session private key corresponding to `session_public_key`. [INV-ATA-01]
- The PoP is an Ed25519 signature by the session private key over the JCS (`ATA-01:02`) serialization of:
  - `typ`: the string `amk-ata-issuance-pop-v1`,
  - `aud`: the Authority's `issuer`,
  - `session_public_key`, `session_handle`, `epoch`, `role`,
  - `request_nonce`, `request_timestamp`.
- The Authority MUST verify the PoP over exactly that value before issuance.
- A failed PoP MUST result in an opaque rejection.

### ATA-02:04 Role Validation

- The Authority MUST verify that the requested role is present in `declared_roles` of the session record. [INV-ATA-02]
- If no session record exists, or the role is absent, the Authority MUST NOT issue.

### ATA-02:05 Requester and Identity Authentication (Profile-Dependent)

- Under the Basic profile, requester authentication is not required.
- Under the Standard profile, the Authority MAY require authentication of the requester or its long-term identity before issuance. Under the High profile it SHOULD.
- When authentication is required, the method MUST be one the metadata lists in `client_authentication`: mutual TLS (RFC 8705) or a JWT client assertion (RFC 7523).
- Long-term identity MUST NOT be included in the attestation. [INV-ATA-03]

### ATA-02:06 Issuance

- On successful validation, the Authority MUST:
  - generate a unique nonce for the attestation,
  - compute `valid_from` and `valid_to` inside the epoch plus grace window (`ATA-01:03`),
  - construct the attestation with all required fields, including the `kid` of an `active` signing key,
  - sign the canonical serialization,
  - return the signed attestation to the requester.
- The Authority MUST refuse to issue if any validation fails.

### ATA-02:07 Binding Rules

- An attestation is bound to:
  - the session public key,
  - the role,
  - the session handle,
  - the epoch.
- A change in any of these requires a new attestation.
- The Authority MUST NOT issue an attestation that conflicts with an existing valid attestation for the same session, epoch, and role unless the existing one is revoked.

### ATA-02:08 Summary

- Issuance requires proof-of-possession, a controller-supplied session record that defines the epoch and the permitted roles, and, under some profiles, requester authentication. The Authority validates the request, checks the role against the session record, and issues a signed attestation bound to the session key, role, session handle, and epoch.

## 03-verification.md

**File:** `03-verification.md`
**Scope:** Verification obligations for clients.
**Reference prefix:** `ATA-03`

---

### ATA-03:01 Verification Obligations

- A client receiving a role attestation MUST verify:
  - the signature, using the key named by `kid` in the signed metadata (`ATA-03:02`),
  - the validity window (`ATA-03:03`),
  - the presentation proof (`ATA-03:04`),
  - the revocation status (`ATA-04`),
  - that the session handle and epoch match the current session context (`ATA-03:06`).
- A client MUST reject an attestation that fails any check.

### ATA-03:02 Signature Verification

- The client MUST obtain the verification key by `kid` from the Authority's signed metadata, verified against the pinned root key (`ATA-01:07`).
- The client MUST reject the attestation if the `kid` is absent from the metadata, the key's status is `revoked`, or the attestation's `valid_from` is outside the key's `not_before` and `not_after`.
- The client MUST verify the signature over the canonical serialization of the attestation fields, using the algorithm the metadata lists for that key.
- A signature verification failure MUST result in opaque denial. [INV-ATA-04]

### ATA-03:03 Validity Window Verification

- The client MUST check that `valid_to` has not passed. No clock tolerance applies to `valid_to`.
- The client MAY allow the clock skew tolerance (`DV-ATA-07`) when checking that `valid_from` has been reached.
- The client MUST reject an attestation outside its validity window.

### ATA-03:04 Presentation Proof

- A verifier MUST obtain proof that the presenter holds the private key for `subject_public_key`.
- The verifier supplies a fresh `verifier_nonce` of at least `DV-ATA-05` bits. It is single-use and expires after `DV-ATA-04`.
- The presenter returns an Ed25519 signature by the session private key over the JCS (`ATA-01:02`) serialization of:
  - `typ`: the string `amk-ata-presentation-v1`,
  - `aud`: the verifier's identifier,
  - `attestation_nonce`: the attestation's `nonce`,
  - `verifier_nonce`,
  - `timestamp`.
- The verifier MUST reject a missing or invalid proof, and a reused or expired `verifier_nonce`.
- The attestation `nonce` identifies the attestation and is not a replay token. The same attestation MAY be presented again with a fresh proof.

### ATA-03:05 Revocation Status

- The client MUST check revocation status on every verification. [INV-ATA-05]
- If the client's revocation list is older than `R_MAX`, or no revocation list is available, it MUST NOT accept the attestation as current.
- The relying party's policy MAY define suspension or restricted handling for that case. Absent such a policy, the client MUST deny.
- Under High, the client MUST also query the real-time status endpoint before relying on the attestation (`ATA-04:04`), and MUST reject it if the endpoint cannot be reached or its answer is not current.

### ATA-03:06 Session Context Verification

- The client MUST verify that the `session_handle` and `epoch` in the attestation match the current session context.
- A mismatch MUST result in opaque denial. [INV-ATA-04]

### ATA-03:07 Controller Behavior

- The controller is a verifying client: it applies ATA-03:01 to ATA-03:06 before relying on an attestation. What a verified attestation permits is decided by controller policy, not by the attestation.
- The controller treats a failed verification as opaque denial. [INV-ATA-04]
- The controller MUST NOT distinguish failure causes to unauthenticated parties.
- The controller MUST log verification failures for audit, and MUST redact secrets and personal data from those logs.

### ATA-03:08 Summary

- Clients verify signature, validity window, presentation proof, revocation status, and session context. A failed verification is opaque denial. Missing or stale revocation data is never treated as "not revoked". Controller behavior follows the same rules.

<a id="file-04-revocation"></a>

## 04-revocation.md

**File:** `04-revocation.md`
**Scope:** Revocation list / real-time revocation.
**Reference prefix:** `ATA-04`

---

### ATA-04:01 Revocation List

- The Authority MUST publish a signed revocation list (RL) under every profile. The list is served by HTTPS GET at `revocation_list_url` from the signed metadata. Distribution is pull-only.
- The RL is a JSON object that MUST contain:
  - `issuer`: the Authority's identifier.
  - `kid`: the key that signed the list.
  - `version`: an integer that increases whenever the list's contents change.
  - `published_at`: the publication timestamp.
  - `revoked`: a list of entries, each with the attestation `nonce`, `revoked_at`, and optionally an opaque `reason` code.
  - `signature`: the signature over the JCS serialization (`ATA-01:02`) without the `signature` field.
- The RL MUST be signed by a key that the signed metadata lists as `active`.
- The RL MUST be republished with a new `published_at` at least every `RL_INTERVAL` (`DV-ATA-06`), even when unchanged.
- An entry MAY be removed once the revoked attestation's `valid_to` has passed.
- Clients MUST reject a list whose `version` is lower than one they have seen.

### ATA-04:02 Revocation Reasons and Requests

- An attestation MAY be revoked for:
  - key compromise,
  - role change,
  - session termination,
  - policy violation,
  - or any other reason defined by the Authority or profile.
- The reason MAY be included in the RL as an opaque code.
- Revocation MUST be requested only by the controller or the Authority's operator over an authenticated channel. Requests from session participants MUST be rejected.
- Revocation never alters or deletes historical audit records (`ATA-06:10`).

### ATA-04:03 Client Obligations

- Clients MUST check the RL on every verification. [INV-ATA-05]
- Clients MUST verify the RL signature using the key named by its `kid` in the signed metadata.
- The age of an RL is the current time minus `published_at`. If it exceeds `R_MAX`, the client MUST NOT accept the attestation as current.
- If no RL is available, the client MUST NOT accept the attestation as current.
- The relying party's policy MAY define suspension or restricted handling for these cases. Absent such a policy, the client MUST deny. This applies to every profile.

### ATA-04:04 Real-Time Revocation Endpoint

- Under the Standard profile, the Authority MAY provide a real-time revocation endpoint. Under the High profile it MUST.
- The endpoint is published as `revocation_status_url` in the signed metadata.
- The endpoint MUST accept a query for a specific attestation `nonce` and return a signed response containing `nonce`, `status` (`good` or `revoked`), `checked_at`, `kid` and `signature`.
- The response MUST be signed by an `active` key listed in the metadata. The client MUST NOT rely on a response older than `DV-ATA-09`.
- Under High, the client MUST query the endpoint when it accepts an attestation, and again whenever its policy re-verifies that attestation. An unreachable endpoint or a response that is not current MUST result in rejection.
- The endpoint MUST be authenticated or rate-limited to prevent abuse.
- Real-time revocation is not offered under the Basic profile.

### ATA-04:05 Revocation Propagation

- The Authority MUST propagate revocations as quickly as the profile allows.
- Worst-case time from revocation to rejection through the RL is about `RL_INTERVAL` plus `R_MAX` (about 360 seconds under Basic and 180 seconds under Standard and High with the default values). Under High the real-time endpoint closes this gap.

### ATA-04:06 Summary

- The Authority publishes a signed, versioned revocation list under every profile. Clients check it on every verification and reject stale, missing, or revoked cases unless policy defines restricted handling. Real-time revocation is optional under Standard and required under High.

<a id="file-05-identity-binding"></a>

## 05-identity-binding.md

**File:** `05-identity-binding.md`
**Scope:** Optional long-term identity binding (profile-dependent).
**Reference prefix:** `ATA-05`

---

### ATA-05:01 Purpose

- Identity binding allows the Authority to associate a session public key with a long-term identity for policy or audit purposes.
- Identity binding is optional and profile-dependent.
- Long-term identity MUST NOT be revealed to session participants. [INV-ATA-03]

### ATA-05:02 When Identity Binding Applies

- Identity binding MAY be required under the Standard profile and SHOULD be required under the High profile.
- Identity binding is not required under the Basic profile.
- The Authority MUST document whether identity binding is enabled and what identity providers are supported.

### ATA-05:03 Binding Process

- When identity binding is required, the Authority MUST authenticate the long-term identity before issuance.
- The authentication method MUST be one listed in the signed metadata (`ATA-02:05`).
- On successful authentication, the Authority MAY store the binding internally.
- The binding MUST NOT be included in the attestation or revealed to any session participant. [INV-ATA-03]

### ATA-05:04 Binding Scope

- Binding scope is per-session. A per-role scope is not supported in this version.
- A change in binding scope requires a new attestation.

### ATA-05:05 Audit

- The Authority MUST audit identity binding events in the format of `ATA-06:10`.
- The audit MUST record the binding, the identity provider, and the authentication method.
- The audit MUST NOT reveal long-term identity to unauthorized parties.

### ATA-05:06 Summary

- Identity binding is optional and profile-dependent. When enabled, the Authority authenticates long-term identity before issuance but never reveals it to session participants. Binding scope is per-session.

<a id="file-06-profiles"></a>

## 06-profiles.md

**File:** `06-profiles.md`
**Scope:** Basic / Standard / High requirements.
**Reference prefix:** `ATA-06`

---

### ATA-06:01 Profile Overview

- The Authority supports three operational profiles:
  - **Basic:** software keys allowed, signed revocation list, suitable for any language.
  - **Standard:** non-exportable keys, full revocation list, process isolation.
  - **High:** HSM/TEE-protected signing keys with published attestation evidence, real-time revocation, hardware-protected audit of issuance.
- The profile in use MUST be documented and published as `profile` in the signed metadata (`ATA-01:07`).

### ATA-06:02 Basic Profile

- Signing keys MAY be software-protected.
- A signed revocation list is required; real-time revocation is not offered.
- Identity binding is not required.
- Process isolation is not required.
- Suitable for development, testing, or low-risk deployments.

### ATA-06:03 Standard Profile

- Signing keys MUST be non-exportable: held by an OS keystore, a key-management service in which the key never leaves the service, or a hardware token. Keys in process memory, files, or environment secrets do not qualify.
- Revocation list is required.
- Real-time revocation endpoint MAY be provided.
- Identity binding MAY be required.
- Process isolation MUST be enforced.
- Suitable for production deployments with moderate security requirements.

### ATA-06:04 High Profile

- Signing keys MUST be HSM- or TEE-protected.
- The metadata MUST include `evidence`: an attestation of the signing-key protection domain, as an Entity Attestation Token (RFC 9711) produced under the RATS architecture (RFC 9334).
- Measured boot of the Authority SHOULD be supported.
- Hardware-protected audit of issuance MUST be enabled.
- Revocation list is required.
- Real-time revocation endpoint MUST be provided.
- Identity binding SHOULD be required.
- Process isolation MUST be enforced.
- Suitable for high-security deployments.

### ATA-06:05 Profile Selection

- The profile MUST be selected at deployment time.
- The profile MUST NOT be changed without re-provisioning.
- Clients MUST read the profile from the signed metadata and verify that it matches their requirements before trusting attestations. If the metadata is unavailable or invalid, the client MUST reject.
- A High claim is accepted only if the client verifies the metadata's `evidence` against a trust anchor provisioned out of band. Otherwise the Authority is treated as no higher than Standard, and a client that requires High MUST reject.
- Basic and Standard claims are assertions by the Authority, not independently verified.

### ATA-06:06 Profile Summary

| Requirement | Basic | Standard | High |
|-------------|-------|----------|------|
| Signing key protection | Software | Non-exportable | HSM/TEE |
| Key-protection evidence in metadata | No | No | Required |
| Revocation list | Required | Required | Required |
| Real-time revocation | No | Optional | Required |
| Identity binding | No | Optional | Should |
| Process isolation | No | Required | Required |
| Measured boot | No | No | Should |
| Hardware audit | No | No | Required |

### ATA-06:07 Peer Profile Rule

- A verifying client or controller MUST NOT accept attestations from an Authority whose profile is lower than the profile the client requires, unless the operator or administrator has explicitly allowed it, and that allowance MUST be audited.
- An Authority whose profile cannot be determined from verified metadata is treated as Basic.

### ATA-06:08 Summary

- Three profiles define the security requirements for the Authority. A signed revocation list is required under all of them. Standard requires non-exportable keys; High adds hardware-protected keys, published attestation evidence, mandatory real-time revocation, and hardware-protected audit. The profile is published in signed metadata and verified by clients.

### ATA-06:09 Declaration and Conformance

- Each implementation MUST document its supported signature algorithms, trust anchors, failure behavior, and any unsupported features.
- Conformance tests MUST cover valid, malformed, expired, revoked, mismatched, and untrusted attestations, and are traced in `amk-test-matrix`.

### ATA-06:10 Audit Record Format and Retention

- The audit trail is JSON Lines (one JSON object per line, UTF-8) and is append-only.
- Each record MUST contain `seq`, `ts` (RFC 3339), `event` (`issue`, `revoke`, `bind`, `key_status`, `metadata_publish`, or `reject`), `outcome`, `prev_hash` and `hash`. It carries `nonce`, `kid`, `session_handle`, `epoch` and `role` where they apply.
- `hash` is the SHA-256 (FIPS 180-4) of the JCS serialization of the record without `hash`, and `prev_hash` is the previous record's `hash`.
- Records MUST NOT contain secrets, private keys, long-term identity, or personal data. [INV-ATA-03]
- An operation that requires an audit record MUST NOT take effect unless that record is committed. If the record cannot be committed, the Authority MUST fail the operation with an opaque error. A record's `outcome` MUST reflect the actual result and MUST NOT be `success` before the operation has taken effect.
- Under High, the trail MUST be protected by hardware and the Authority MUST periodically sign a checkpoint of `seq` and `hash` with a signing key.
- Minimum retention is stated in `DV-ATA-10`. Revocation never alters or deletes audit records.

### ATA-99:01 Open Items

- **O-ATA-01** Multiple simultaneous signing keys for rotation. RESOLVED: a signed key set with `kid` and key statuses (`ATA-01:07`).
- **O-ATA-02** Format of the session's declared role list and how it reaches the Authority. RESOLVED: the controller supplies it in the session record (`ATA-02:02`).
- **O-ATA-03** Whether identity binding is per-role or per-session. RESOLVED: per-session (`ATA-05:04`).
- **O-ATA-04** Revocation list distribution mechanism. RESOLVED: pull-only by HTTPS GET (`ATA-04:01`).
- **O-ATA-05** How the Authority is enrolled with the controller at first use. RESOLVED: out-of-band pinning at deployment of the root key (`ATA-01:07`) and the controller identity (`ATA-02:02`); there is no online enrollment handshake.
- **O-ATA-06** Delegation of issuance to sub-authorities. CLOSED: not supported in this version; the Authority is the sole issuer (`ATA-00:01`).
- **O-ATA-07** PoP challenge format. RESOLVED: a deterministic signed value, with no challenge round-trip (`ATA-02:03`).
- **O-ATA-08** Metadata at a well-known endpoint. RESOLVED (`ATA-01:07`).
- **O-ATA-09** Audit format and retention per profile. RESOLVED (`ATA-06:10`, `DV-ATA-10`).
- **O-ATA-10** Batch issuance for multiple roles. CLOSED: not supported in this version; one request yields one attestation (`ATA-02:06`).
- **O-ATA-11** OPEN. The controller-to-Authority interface that carries the session record and revocation requests: channel, authentication of the controller, delivery and error semantics. Cross-spec with `amk-controller`; to be indexed in `specs/core/OPEN-ITEMS.md`.

### ATA-99:02 Blocking Items

- `O-ATA-11` is blocking for a 1.2.0 release.
- All other items are resolved or closed.

### ATA-99:03 Summary

- Ten of eleven open items are resolved or closed. The remaining item is the controller-to-Authority interface for the session record and revocation requests.
